"On a patient-grouped 5-source kidney CT dataset (v3-corrected: 8,924 images, 561 patients), we tested six architectural and training-time interventions targeting source-shortcut learning: focal loss with class-balanced sampling, adversarial source removal (DANN), test-time augmentation with 5 transforms, and multi-head architectures with auxiliary segmentation using three Cyst source-gating variants. Under Bonferroni correction across 36 tests (α_corrected = 0.0014), not a single intervention produced improvement over a well-tuned single-head ResNet50 baseline. DANN significantly degraded performance (−8.5pp accuracy, p=0.001; −9.6pp Tumor F1, p<0.001), indicating that the source-related features it removes carry classification-relevant signal. The remaining five interventions produced null results.

Combined with our v2 finding that CBAM attention and Vision Transformer architectures also underperform Plain ResNet50, we conclude that architectural and loss-function sophistication is not the primary lever for performance on this multi-source kidney CT classification task. The bottleneck is representational: distinguishing cystic, tumorous, and normal kidney parenchyma under multi-source imaging protocols is fundamentally difficult, and no tested modification of model capacity, class weighting, adversarial regularisation, inference-time averaging, or per-class output head design substantially changes this."
